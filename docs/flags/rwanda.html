Flag of RwandaRepubulika y'u Rwanda

Blue, yellow and green bands with a golden sun — the flag of the Republic of Rwanda since 2001.

Quick answer: The flag of Rwanda has four horizontal bands: blue (top, half the width), yellow (quarter) and two equal green bands (quarter). A golden sun with 24 rays sits in the upper hoist corner of the blue band. The proportion is 2:3.

What the flag looks like

The Rwandan flag consists of four horizontal bands: blue (top, half the width), yellow (quarter) and two green bands (each eighth of the width). In the upper right corner of the blue band is a golden sun with 24 rays. The proportion is 2:3. It was adopted on 25 October 2001, replacing the flag used since 1961, which had been associated with the 1994 Rwandan genocide.

Colors and meaning

Blue represents peace and happiness and the Rwandan people's hope for a bright future, yellow represents economic development and mineral wealth, and green represents prosperity and the country's natural resources. The golden sun represents enlightenment, unity and the fight against ignorance.

Blue

Represents peace, happiness and the Rwandan people's hope for a peaceful and prosperous future, especially after the 1994 genocide against the Tutsi.

Yellow

Represents economic development and mineral wealth. Rwanda has significant mineral resources, including tin, tungsten and gold.

Green

Represents prosperity and the country's natural resources and agriculture. Rwanda is known as the 'Land of a Thousand Hills' for its mountainous terrain and rich vegetation.

Golden sun

Represents enlightenment, unity and the fight against ignorance. The 24 rays of the sun represent the unity of the Rwandan people and their collective effort to build a new nation after the genocide.

Symbolic break from the past

The flag was designed to be completely different from the previous flag, which had been associated with the genocide and the political divisions that led to it. The new design emphasizes unity, peace and a shared national identity.

History and adoption

Rwanda gained independence from Belgium on 1 July 1962 and adopted a flag with vertical bands of red, yellow and green and a black 'K' in the center, representing the Hutu-led government. This flag remained in use until 2001, including through the 1994 genocide against the Tutsi, in which around 800,000 people were killed in about 100 days. After the genocide, the new Rwandan government under the Rwandan Patriotic Front (RPF) decided to create a new flag to symbolize a break with the past. The current design was adopted on 25 October 2001 and has remained unchanged since.

Historical and related flags

Rwanda has used two flags since independence, with the current design adopted to break from the past.

  • German East Africa (1894–1916): German colonial flags.
  • Belgian Ruanda-Urundi (1916–1962): Belgian colonial flags.
  • First Rwandan flag (1962–2001): Red, yellow and green vertical bands with a black K in the center.
  • Current flag (2001–present): Blue, yellow and green bands with a golden sun.
  • Presidential standard: Uses the Rwandan coat of arms on a blue field.
