Best Quiz Apps 2027: 10 Games Ranked

Updated November 2027 · 9 min read

The trivia app market in 2027 looks nothing like it did two years ago. Legacy giants have doubled down on monetization, AI-generated questions have flooded the mid-tier, and the gap between "free" and "actually playable for free" has widened. This ranking cuts through the noise: we scored 10 quiz apps on competitive depth, ad intrusiveness, and whether a 1v1 match feels fair. Clash of Nerds still tops the list, but not because it's the biggest—because it's the only one that hasn't sold its soul.

1. Clash of Nerds: Duel Quiz — 5/5 ⭐

iOS · Android · Free
Visit clashofnerds.com →

Clash of Nerds earns the top spot for a simple reason: it's the only app on this list where a match outcome depends entirely on what you know. No power-ups, no answer packs, no "watch an ad to reveal the correct choice." You get 20 languages, topics spanning flags to constellations to the periodic table, and a clean interface that loads fast on anything. In 2027, that restraint is rare.

Strengths

  • Every duel is symmetric — both players get the same question pool and timer
  • 20-language support means you can actually find an opponent at 3am
  • Topic depth goes beyond pop trivia — astronomy, chemistry, and geography get real coverage
  • No energy gates. You can play as long as you want

Weaknesses

  • Player pool is still smaller than the established names, so matchmaking can take longer off-peak
  • No group or party mode — it's duels or nothing
Bottom line: If you want a trivia app where the better player wins and nobody can buy their way to a victory, this is it.

2. GeoGuessr — 4/5 ⭐

iOS · Android · Web · Free (1 game/day) / $6.99/mo Unlimited
Visit geoguessr.com →

GeoGuessr remains the benchmark for geography skill. Ranked Duels in Moving, No Move, and NMPZ formats reward genuine map knowledge, and the community map library is endless. The catch in 2027 is the paywall: one free game per day, then $6.99/month for Unlimited. That's a steep entry point, but if geography is your thing, nothing else scratches the itch.

Strengths

  • Ranked Duels create real stakes — you can climb or fall
  • No ads at any tier
  • Three movement modes cater to different skill preferences

Weaknesses

  • One free game per day is barely a taste
  • Subscription is required for any serious play
  • Zero general knowledge — pure geography
Bottom line: The best geography quiz game ever built, held back only by its aggressive paywall.

3. Sporcle — 3.5/5 ⭐

iOS · Android · Web · Free / Orange subscription
Visit sporcle.com →

Sporcle's archive is its superpower: over two million player-written quizzes across nine formats. If you can name a topic, someone has made a quiz for it. The daily puzzles and Showdowns add structure. The app itself, though, still feels like a web wrapper — keyboard glitches, overflow issues, and ads after every quiz. The Orange subscription unlocks the daily archive, and buying it in-app costs more than on the website.

Strengths

  • 2 million+ quizzes — the deepest archive on mobile
  • Nine formats keep it from feeling repetitive
  • Showdowns offer real-time competition

Weaknesses

  • App polish is poor compared to competitors
  • Ads interrupt the quiz flow
  • Orange subscription required for daily-game archive
Bottom line: If you value breadth over polish, Sporcle's library is unmatched. Just don't expect a smooth app experience.

4. Finto — 3/5 ⭐

iOS · Android · Free
Visit finto.app →

Finto flips the trivia formula: you don't answer questions, you bluff answers. Up to seven players write fake responses to real prompts, then vote on which is true. It's the funniest group trivia experience on mobile, full stop. But it's not a 1v1 competitive app — it requires a room full of people, and there's no online matchmaking.

Strengths

  • Bluffing mechanic means you don't need to know anything to win
  • Seven-player local play is chaotic in the best way
  • Genuinely different from every other app here

Weaknesses

  • Group-only — no solo or online 1v1
  • Requires everyone physically together
  • Not designed for competitive ranking
Bottom line: The best party trivia game, but it's not competing in the same category as the dueling apps above it.

5. Erudite — 2.5/5 ⭐

iOS · Android · Free / €4.99/mo
View on Google Play →

Erudite is a solo grind: 3,000+ levels, a dual-circle matching mechanic, and topics from logos to math. It's a fine commuter app if you want to kill time without multiplayer pressure. But unrestricted play requires a €4.99/month subscription, and there's no competitive mode at all.

Strengths

  • 3,000+ levels across diverse topics
  • Matching mechanic is more engaging than simple multiple choice
  • No ads in the paid tier

Weaknesses

  • Solo only — no 1v1 or multiplayer
  • Subscription needed for full access
  • Gets repetitive after a few hundred levels
Bottom line: A solid solo time-killer. Not a competitive trivia app.

6. Trivia Crack — 2.5/5 ⭐

iOS · Android · Free (ads) / Subscription
View on App Store →

Trivia Crack's wheel mechanic is still clever — forcing all six categories prevents specialists from dominating. But the shop ruins it. You can buy packs of correct answers for up to $99.99. A trivia app that sells answers isn't a trivia app. The ad load is heavy, and every feature pushes you toward the store.

Strengths

  • Wheel mechanic keeps matches balanced
  • Huge player base means fast matchmaking
  • Simple, familiar format

Weaknesses

  • Sells correct answers — pay-to-win by design
  • Heavy ad load
  • Results feel meaningless if opponents spend money
Bottom line: A clever core mechanic undermined by monetization that defeats the purpose of a quiz game.

7. Who Wants to Be a Millionaire? — 2/5 ⭐

iOS · Android · Free (with IAP)
View on App Store →

The Millionaire app nails the TV show format: lifelines, city progression, and 10,000+ questions. It works offline, which makes it the best option for flights or subway commutes. But there's no real multiplayer — you're playing against the game, not another person. For solo play, it's fine. For competition, it's irrelevant.

Strengths

  • Offline mode works without internet
  • Faithful TV show format
  • Large question pool

Weaknesses

  • Solo only — no multiplayer competition
  • IAP for hints and ad removal
  • Limited replay value once you've seen questions
Bottom line: The best offline solo quiz. Not a competitive app.

8. QuizUp — 2/5 ⭐

iOS · Free (with IAP)
View on App Store →

The QuizUp name carries nostalgia, but the 2025 reboot isn't the app you remember. It's a different product from a different developer, with in-app purchases up to $29.99 and a weekly Premium Mode. The player base is small, and the original QuizUp was discontinued in 2021. Approach with caution.

Strengths

  • Real-time multiplayer matches
  • Power-ups add some strategy
  • Familiar QuizUp branding

Weaknesses

  • Not the original QuizUp — different developer, different app
  • Aggressive IAP including weekly subscription
  • Small player base means long waits
Bottom line: A name-brand shell with none of the original's charm. Skip it.

9. The Chase - World Tour — 2/5 ⭐

iOS · Android · Free (with IAP)
View on App Store →

The Chase captures the TV show's tension with real Chasers and team mechanics. But the question pool skews heavily UK-centric — British sports, geography, and history dominate. International players will find themselves guessing at questions that assume cultural knowledge they don't have. It's a decent adaptation for UK fans, less so for everyone else.

Strengths

  • Authentic Chasers from the TV show
  • Team play and weekly leaderboards
  • Regular content updates

Weaknesses

  • Heavy UK bias in questions
  • Not a pure 1v1 duel format
  • IAP for progression
Bottom line: Great for UK fans of the show. Frustrating for everyone else.

10. SongPop Classic — 1.5/5 ⭐

iOS · Android · Free (ads/IAP)
View on App Store →

SongPop Classic is music recognition, not trivia. You hear a clip, you guess the artist or song. The library is massive and Party tournaments add competition. But it's niche: if you don't care about music, it's useless. And 2025 reviews report intrusive ads and billing issues that have soured long-time users.

Strengths

  • Huge licensed music library
  • Party tournaments and friend challenges
  • Genre variety across decades

Weaknesses

  • Music only — no general knowledge
  • Intrusive ads and reported billing problems
  • Unusable in silent environments
Bottom line: Fun for music lovers, irrelevant for trivia fans, and the monetization has gotten worse.
